# Big Aggregated Numbers (BANs)

Big Aggregated Numbers (BANs) are single, straightforward values that show the most important insight from a dataset at a glance. Their main purpose is to give information quickly. BANs remove extra detail so readers can understand the key message without looking at a full chart or table.

In reports, a BAN is often placed in a shaded box, a sidebar, or at the start of a section to draw attention to an important finding.

BANs provide simplicity and clarity. By reducing complex data to one number, they help readers understand the big picture fast. They can also help with comparisons or benchmarking when you add short text or context, such as last year’s value.

BANs work best when they are supported by a short explanation that gives more detail or nuance

## Practical examples of BANs

### Conflict intensity

- 1,245 conflict‑related fatalities recorded nationwide in 2025.
- 72% of all violent incidents concentrated in three districts.
- Number of security incidents is 24% higher than the 12-month average.

### Security trends

- 23‑day period without recorded clashes. The longest lull in two years.
- 41% decline in cross‑line movement disruptions compared to last quarter.

### Access constraints & humanitarian impact

- 27% of planned humanitarian missions denied or delayed.
- 112 security incidents affecting aid operations this year.[IN1.1]

## When to use BANs instead of charts

BANs should be used over charts when:

- Your key message is a single data point, or one important fact. 
- You want to communicate an outcome, instead of a relationship.
- You want to provide visual simplicity or break up an already dense piece of text.
- You can provide additional context in the surrounding text.

## When NOT to Use BANs

BANs must not be used when:

- The reader needs context from several data points to understand the message
- The insight depends on showing relationships, such as comparisons, distributions, or how different parts of a whole.

# Guidelines for including BANs in your reports
BANs should use brand colours. Red and green may be used *sparingly* as positive/negative trend indicators
					
- BANs must be easy to interpret. They are simple by design and so must be immediately understandable. Indicate units, timeframes, and relevance clearly. 
- BANs should be used occasionally. Too many BANs dilute their impact and can overwhelm the reader. Choose only the numbers that genuinely distil the core message.
- BANs should be placed where they naturally support the narrative structure. This is often early in a section or at the margin of a page. 
- Numbers used in BANs should be contextualised if possible by providing familiar perspectives: 
    * “Equivalent to X% of Y”
    *  “About the same as X”
    * “Roughly X times higher/lower than last year/quarter.” 
    * “Equivalent to about one X for every Y”
