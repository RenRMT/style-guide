# Chorochromatic maps

Chorochromatic maps are useful when you want to show differences in type rather than amount. They work best with nominal categories that have no natural order, such as land use types, ethnic groups, administrative areas, or the presence of different armed actors. To avoid suggesting hierarchy or quantity, these maps rely on distinct colours or textures instead of changes in lightness or saturation. 

You should keep the number of categories small, make sure they do not overlap, and choose colour schemes that do not imply ranking. When a map includes more than two variables, extra symbols can help if they do not create visual clutter. 

In cases where natural or administrative boundaries make patterns hard to interpret, a hexbin version of a chorochromatic map can provide a clearer, more regular structure.

## Examples:
- Territorial control / areas of influence
- Ethnic or religious group presence, 
- Humanitarian access conditions.
