# Presentation

A visualisation has **clear presentation** when the reader can quickly understand its structure, relationships, and meaning. It follows good perceptual design principles that reduce the effort needed to understand the visualisation. It is intuitive, readable, and free of unnecessary clutter.

## Examples of visuals with clear presentation

	A line chart with clear labelling, and well placed legends, allowing viewers to understand trends immediately.
	A bar chart with logical grouping and direct labelling, making comparisons easy.
	A map that uses logical colour ramps, intuitive legends, and clear labels so that spatial patterns are easy to recognise.
	A map designed with good visual hierarchy, where important features stand out immediately, and supporting features are given less emphasis.

## Examples of visuals with presentation issues

A visualisation has presentation issues when it creates perceptual problems. It may be unclear, confusing, overly complex, or visually misleading (even if the underlying data is correct). Poor design choices can distort how viewers understand relationships or magnitudes.

	A choropleth map with a perceptually misleading colour gradient, such as nonlinear hues.
	A map with poor symbol hierarchy where unimportant features are drawn more prominent than important ones.
	A pie chart with many categories of similar size, making it difficult to compare values.
