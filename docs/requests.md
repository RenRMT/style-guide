# Making a good data visualisation request 

To make a good data visualisation request, you need to have a clear picture of what you want to visualise. A simple way to develop this is to consider the Who, Why, What, When, Where and How (5 Ws and 1 H). If you can provide the answers to these questions to the data viz team, they can help you create a data visualisation with purpose, good structure, good presentation, and a polished style. If you are unsure about any aspect, discuss your idea with the data viz team for guidance.

Start by identifying **who** the product is for. In most cases, the audience will be partners, though some visuals are intended for internal use. Knowing the audience upfront helps determine how much detail is needed and how technical the content should be.

Next, be clear about **why** the visual is needed. State the objective of the request and focus on the single most important message the audience needs to understand. Avoid trying to show everything at once. A good visual answers one core question clearly.

Then describe **what** you want the visual to show. Explain the key information you want to communicate, such as a comparison, a trend, or background context. Provide a clear title and any essential explanatory context that will help readers understand the data. If certain symbols, icons, or visual elements are required, mention them, while recognizing that final design choices can be made by the data viz team. Check that your analysis or our data supports the message you want the visual to convey and that the trend or pattern is visible.

Specify **when** the data applies by clearly defining the date range or reporting period. Time filters help show relevance, allow trends to be identified, and support correct interpretation, especially for reports or recurring products.

Explain **where** the visual applies by defining the geographic scope. For our use, this means specifying the relevant geographic coverage. For incident maps, note the required database filters and administrative levels, such as state, region, or district. For contextual maps, clearly describe the locations and associated data points, for example population displacement areas or movement from one town to another during a specific period. If additional elements are needed, such as arrows, road labels, village names, icons, or coordinates, state these clearly.

Finally, consider **how** to visualise the data in a way that is easy to understand. Make sure you understand the data and the message yourself before submitting the request. Clear thinking at this stage helps others translate your request into a visual that is accurate, readable, and useful. 

## Checklist
### Who
- Have I identified the primary audience (partners or internal users)?
- Is the level of detail appropriate for that audience?
### Why
- Is the purpose of the visual clearly stated?
- Have I identified the single most important message or question the visual should answer?
### What
- Have I clearly described the data or information to be shown?
- Does the request explain whether the visual shows a comparison, trend, or context?
- Have I provided a clear title for the visual?
- Have I included any necessary background or contextual information?
- Have I noted any specific symbols, icons, or visual elements needed?
- Does the data or our database analysis clearly support the message I want the visual to show?
### When
- Have I specified the date range or period?
- Is it clear whether the visual shows a snapshot or change over time?
### Where
- Have I defined the geographic scope of the visual?
- For incident maps, have I specified database filters and admin levels (state, region, district, etc.)?
- For contextual maps, have I clearly described the locations and relevant data points?
- Have I listed any required extra elements (arrows, labels, routes, villages, icons, coordinates)?
### How
- Is the requested visualisation type clear and easy to understand?
- Do I personally understand the data and message well enough to explain it simply?
- Have I discussed with the data viz team about alternative visualisation types?
