# Typeface requirements

The choice for a typeface to be used in data visualisations should serve the purpose of communicating the key message clearly. There are three general criteria a typeface must meet for this:

1.	Consistency: typeface must be standard installed on windows devices and software used.
2.	Legibility: typeface must render well in different sizes, in print and digital.
3.	Multiple weights: typeface must have multiple weights available. This helps in emphasising the key points of the visual, and de-empahsising supporting text elements.

Because data visualisations are to a larger extent concerned with the display of numbers compared to regular texare some special considerations for the display of numerals. These are:

1.	Lining: lining numerals align on the baseline and share a uniform height
2.	Tabulating: tabular numerals all occupy the same horizontal space
3.	Multiplexing: multiplexed typefaces are typeface families where different styles (regular, light, bold) all have the same numeral width. 

Sans serif fonts are generally considered to be more legible at smaller sizes, as well as specifically when display on screens. Although there has been a long-standing and related belief that serif fonts display better in print, but there is little evidence to support this. This suggests that a choice for a sans serif font to support digital display will not harm the legibility of visualisations displayed in print.

There are five sans serif typefaces that are installed om most windows machines and therefore meet the availablity and legibility criteria: Aptos, Arial, Calibri, Segoe UI, and Verdana. Of these five fonts Aptos and Calibri are the only two that meet all criteria for the display of numbers. Of these two, Aptos offers a wider selection of font weights, most notably it includes a regular, light and semibold font weight. Calibri in contrast offers only a regular and light font weight, but the regular font can be bolded (Ctrl+B) in most applications. The Calibri typeface is also notably narrower than Aptos, this can be an advantage because it means more text will fit on the visualisation without needing to reduce the font size, improving legibility. 

As a final consideration, Aptos is now the default font selected in most office aplication, but Calibri would be consistent with the broader brand style guidelines. For the above reason, Calibri will be chosen as the typeface for all data visualisations because it better meets two of the three criteria outlined above (consistency and legibility).
